Before anything else, read `00 Start Here.md`, `01 Planning/Decisions.md`, `01 Planning/Book Status.md` and the last five lines of `01 Planning/Session Log.md`. Follow every rule in CLAUDE.md. Do this one job only. Also read `01 Planning/Book Brief.md`.

Job: build or refresh `Research/Comparable Books.md`. Use web search. If no search tool is available, say so and stop.

1. **Find ten to twelve books** in the same space: the classics the reader already knows, the recent books on the same problem, and the books on the method or tools the book uses. Ask the author for any they have in mind first.
2. **For each book, from the publisher's or author's own description** (open the page; cite it with the date you opened it): title, author, year, publisher, who it's written for, what it covers.
3. **Then our reading:** what it does well, and the opening it leaves for our reader. These come from the descriptions, not the full books, so mark the section "check against the book before anything goes into print".
4. **Tone:** never criticize another book. Credit what each does well, then describe the difference as an opportunity for our reader, never as a flaw in theirs. Use no sales figures or bestseller rankings unless they can be verified.
5. **Close with a summary:** the gap the book fills that none of the others does, in two or three sentences, ready for the Book Brief and a proposal.
6. Write the page with properties (`type: research`, `tags: [book, research]`, `updated`) and a sources list.

When finished, update `01 Planning/Book Status.md` and `00 Start Here.md`, and add one line to `01 Planning/Session Log.md` (date | job | what was done | where it stopped | next). Then tell the author in three lines or fewer what you did and what comes next.
